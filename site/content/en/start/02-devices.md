# Devices and the app

*One journal on the phone, the tablet and the computer.*

## Install it as an app

On a phone, the agenda works best from the home screen. In Safari on an iPhone: share button → *Add to Home Screen*. In Chrome on Android: menu → *Install app*. On a computer, Chrome and Edge offer *Install* in the address bar. The app then opens in its own window, without the browser's toolbars.

## A second device

On the other device, open the same address and choose *I have an account phrase*. Enter your eight words. Instead of typing them, you can show a QR code on the first device (*Settings → Journals → Edit → Show QR code*) and scan it on the second one.

Journals you add later appear on all your devices after their next sync.

## Sync

The two circular arrows at the top synchronise; they turn while a sync runs and turn red if it failed. The app also synchronises by itself when you change something and once a minute while it is open. Below the header, *in sync* and the time tell when the last sync succeeded.

## Without network

The app keeps a copy of your journals in the browser and works offline. What you change is sent when the server can be reached again.

## The clipboard

The tray left of the magnifier moves one thing between your own devices: a picture, a file or a text, up to 4 MB. Drop it on the tray, or click the tray and paste with Ctrl/Cmd+V, or choose a file; on the phone, tap the tray and *Paste* or *Choose file or photo*. The tray turns blue on your other devices at their next synchronisation. There, a click copies a text or a picture into the clipboard; dragging the tray into a folder saves a copy; on an iPhone, *Save / share* and *Save Image* put a photo into Photos. A new item replaces the old, *Clear* removes it, and after 24 hours the server removes it. Only you can read it; those you share journals with see nothing of it.

## A shared device

On a device that several people use, a tablet in the kitchen for example, the PIN can be switched off: *Settings → Account → PIN → No PIN*. The device then keeps everything unencrypted, and whoever uses it can read and pass on what it holds. Give such a device only guest codes, or ally codes where people should write, never the words of a journal. Its own journal is then the only one it owns.

## Clearing a device

*Settings → Clear data in this browser* removes the words and the copy of the journal from this browser. The journal on the server is not touched.
