# What it is

*Events, tasks and notes in one journal — and why that is enough.*

## The idea

A planning journal in the old sense: one book where everything goes that concerns the coming days. The agenda keeps this form and adds what paper cannot do: it sorts by date, filters by area of life, repeats what repeats, and is the same on every device.

Three kinds of entries share the list:

- **Events** belong to their day and time: a meeting, a visit, a birthday.
- **Tasks** move along until they are done: writing an offer, calling someone back. A task that is not done today is there again tomorrow.
- **Notes** are kept to be looked up later: an address, a customs number, a recipe. They are never due and stay out of the daily list.

The difference lies in a tag, not in a separate program. An event can become a task by removing its time; a note can get a date.

## Facets

Every entry belongs to an area of your life or work, a *facet*: `work`, `family`, `garden`. Facets give the entries their colour and let you show or hide whole areas with one tap. You choose your own; the app starts with two.

## Journals

Entries live in journals. You have one of your own; more can be added: a journal for the family, one for a club, one that someone else shares with you. The list shows all of them together, and every entry stays in the journal it belongs to.

## What it is not

The agenda does not send reminders, does not invite people by e-mail and does not analyse what you write. It is a journal: it shows what you put in, in the order of time, and keeps it confidential.
